Framing and the Five Refinement Moves

MC1 · 1.2 Framing and Refining · Use an approved AI tool to complete a routine task and check the result before using it

Practical takeaway State your context, role, task and format before you ask an AI for anything. Then use five short follow-up moves — narrow, expand, change register, challenge and merge — to fix what is wrong with the first draft, instead of starting over.

1. Build a request that works the first time

A request works when it states four things: context, role, task and format. Leave one out, and the AI has to guess — and a guess about a date, a penalty, a policy or a safety step is not something to hand to a trainee without checking.

Weak request

“Write about lathe safety.”

Usable request

“You are a workshop facilitator. Write a 100-word safety reminder for first-year ITI trainees, to read out before the lathe machine practical. Format it as three short points for the noticeboard.”

Context

What is this for, and who will read or use it?

Role

Who should the AI write as — a facilitator, a class representative, a shift supervisor?

Task

What should it actually produce — a reminder, a notice, a summary?

Format

How should the output look — a noticeboard point, an email, a table?

Indian context lane

In a workshop setting, this could be a maintenance log summary or a work-order handover for the next batch. In a campus setting, it could be a project submission notice or a study group plan.

Use fictional names, dates and figures in practice, and label them as fictional. Never use real trainee records, marks or contact details.

2. Five moves to refine the draft

A first draft is rarely final. Instead of rewriting the whole request, use one of these five moves to fix exactly what is wrong with it.

1

Narrow

Fixes an answer that tries to cover too much.

“Focus only on this part.”
2

Expand

Fixes an answer that skips detail you need.

“Add more detail on this part.”
3

Change register

Fixes a tone or reading level that does not suit the audience.

“Rewrite this for [audience].”
4

Challenge

Fixes a claim that sounds confident but is unverified.

“What could be wrong here, and what have you assumed?”
5

Merge

Fixes strong points scattered across two drafts.

“Combine the best of this draft and the earlier one.”
Stop rule

Stop refining once fixing it yourself — a date, a name, a line of formatting — is faster or safer than sending another request. Refining is a tool, not a target.

Practice

Try this

You are a class representative. A group assignment is due Friday (a fictional date, for practice). Write one message to your study group stating context, role, task and format together. Then ask the AI to narrow the message to a short chat length, and challenge it to flag any date or instruction it added on its own.

Check before use

  • Did the AI add a date, number, policy or safety step you did not give it? Confirm it before use.
  • Does the format match what will actually be printed, posted or sent?
  • Would someone outside this conversation understand it without help?
  • Is every name, figure and document in the example fictional or already verified?

Five-line recap

  1. State context, role, task and format before you ask.
  2. Narrow a draft that is too broad; expand one that is too thin.
  3. Change register for the wrong tone, and challenge a claim you cannot verify.
  4. Merge the strongest parts when good material is split across drafts.
  5. Stop refining once a manual fix is faster — and check before you use it.
